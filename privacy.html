privacy

updated 2026-10-27

Mithraeum makes hako (an AI agent for the terminal), hake (an editor), hakm (an engine that runs models on your own computer) and hako studio (hako in a browser, at hako.mithraeum.studio). they run on your devices. this page says what reaches us, which is very little, and what we keep.

the short version

  • no analytics, no tracking, no ads, no cookies
  • your chats, files, prompts and provider keys stay on your devices. we never receive them
  • an account is optional. it is only there so your own devices can reach each other, and it keeps no name, no email address and no content

the tools

hako, hake and hakm send nothing to us. when you use a cloud model, hako sends your prompts from your device straight to the provider you chose (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter and others), with your key or your sign-in there, and that provider's privacy policy covers it. a model run by hakm on your own computer sends nothing anywhere. hako contacts GitHub when you ask it to update itself or to install a skill.

hako studio runs in your browser. your chats, settings and keys are kept in that browser's storage on your device. we can't read them, and clearing the site's data in your browser deletes them.

accounts

an account (invite only, during the beta) lets your phone or another browser open a hako session running on your computer. you sign in with a passkey, GitHub, Google or a code sent to your email. for that we keep:

  • a random account id
  • for a passkey: its public key
  • for GitHub or Google: a keyed hash of the account id GitHub or Google gives us. never your name, email address, picture or a token. we ask GitHub for no permissions and Google for openid only
  • for email: a keyed hash of the address. the address is used to send the code and is not stored
  • your account key, locked by your passkey, if you choose to keep it that way. we can't unlock it
  • for each of your devices: its public key, and its name sealed with your account key, so we can't read it
  • used invite codes, as hashes, not linked to any account

we keep no sign-in history, no dates and no IP addresses.

google and github sign-in

when you sign in with Google, we ask for the openid scope only, so Google gives us one thing: a fixed id for your Google account. we use it for one purpose, to recognize you the next time you sign in. the relay turns it into a keyed hash at once and keeps only the hash; the token Google sends is thrown away as soon as the id is read. we don't receive your name, email address or picture, and we don't ask for your Gmail, Drive, contacts, calendar or anything else.

we never share, sell or transfer what Google gives us, never use it for ads, and never use it to train or improve AI models. Mithraeum's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

GitHub works the same way: we ask for no permissions, read only your account's numeric id, keep only its keyed hash, and throw the token away. deleting your account (below) deletes these hashes.

the relay

what your devices send each other passes through relay.mithraeum.studio, encrypted end to end with keys only your devices hold. we can't read it and we don't store it: a message for a device that is offline is dropped. while you're connected, the relay sees your IP address and the timing and size of what passes through, as any server does. it doesn't log them.

you can run the relay yourself instead: it is open source, and one line in ~/.hakorc points hako at it.

others involved

  • GitHub Pages hosts mithraeum.studio and hako.mithraeum.studio. GitHub may log visits, as its privacy statement describes
  • the relay runs on a server we rent from Amazon Web Services
  • if you sign in with GitHub or Google, they know you signed in to Mithraeum
  • when we email you a sign-in code, the mail service that delivers it handles the address to do so

we don't sell, share or rent anything, and there is nothing to sell: we don't have it.

deleting

signing out on a device removes that device from your account. to delete the account itself, write to contact@mithraeum.studio and we'll remove it and every device in it.

children

Mithraeum is not meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly hold anything about them.

changes

if this page changes, so does the date at the top. every earlier version stays in the site's history on GitHub.
